Issue 12
Nov 20, 2026
Submarket spotlight

If your 250,000 SF-plus warehouse burns, nobody insures the year AB 98 can add.

Gov. Code 65098.1(a) has required, since January 1, 2026, that a proposed new or expanded logistics development of 250,000 square feet or more on industrial land with a loading bay within 900 feet of a sensitive receptor orient its truck bays away from receptors where feasible and set them at least 300 feet from the nearest receptor’s property line, with Tier 1 building standards attached; a home is a sensitive receptor under 65098(e). Section 65098.1.5(a)(1) shields a development in existence as of September 30, 2024 if a receptor is built or permitted later. Nothing in the AB 98 text or in the 2025 cleanup bill as amended September 9, 2025 mentions reconstruction, rebuild, damage, destruction, fire or casualty, so whether a demolish-and-rebuild is a “proposed new” development that loses the shield is unresolved, with no city determination, opinion or case on it. On a 300,000 square foot South Ontario building, a 14-month code-free rebuild plus a 12-month AB 98 redesign is 26 months of downtime against a landlord Rental Value policy that runs 12 months plus a 180-day extended period, and 12 months of stopgap rent at Lee’s Q3 2026 IE West average NNN of $1.034 is $3,722,400 that no policy covers: $12.41 a foot, $2.48 a foot a year spread over a five-year term, 20.0% of a year’s rent. The gap is structural on both sides. ISO CP 00 30 says the period of restoration “does not include any increased period required due to the enforcement of any ordinance or law” regulating construction, use or repair, and the AIR single-tenant net lease caps abatement at the Rental Value proceeds the landlord collects (¶9.6(a) against ¶8.3(b)), so the added year lands on the tenant twice, as stopgap rent and as rent on the burned building. The fix on the insurance side is ISO CP 15 31, Ordinance or Law – Increased Period of Restoration, which reaches laws in force at the time of loss, and AB 98’s standards have been in force since January 1, 2026. One condition governs the double rent: unamended, ¶9.1(b) calls damage that cannot reasonably be repaired in six months a Premises Total Destruction and ¶9.4 ends the lease 60 days later, so the two-rent case needs a lease that keeps the tenant in place, and where the lease does terminate the business-income gap remains. April 7, 2026 showed the stopgap in this market: fire destroyed the 1.2 million square foot Kimberly-Clark distribution center in Ontario, and NFI Industries took 423,665 square feet at 20123 Harvill Avenue in Perris on an 18-month term at $0.83 NNN. The issue gives six asks in the order to make them, with sample abatement language, and runs the sensitivity: $2,988,000 at the Perris rate, $1,861,200 on a six-month delay, $5,583,600 on eighteen.

Issue 11
Nov 13, 2026
Lease economics

Sell your company without landlord consent and your free rent can come back in full.

Four months free on a 200,000 square foot Inland Empire West renewal, priced at Lee’s 2026 medians for deals of 100,000 square feet and above, $1.00 a foot a month NNN on a 64-month term, is $800,000 of abated rent, or $4.00 a foot. On the AIR net lease forms that abatement is conditional. Paragraph 12.1 treats a cumulative 25% shift in voting control, a merger or buyout, or any financing that cuts the tenant’s net worth by more than a quarter as an assignment needing the landlord’s written consent, unless the tenant’s stock trades on a national exchange, so a sale at the parent or at a private-equity sponsor is a deemed assignment even though the tenant never assigns or sublets anything. Paragraph 12.1(d) lets the landlord, not the tenant, decide whether an unconsented transfer is a curable Default or a non-curable Breach that needs no notice and no grace period, and on a Breach the landlord may terminate or lift Base Rent to 110% for the balance of the term: $893,607 across months 25 to 64 on a month-24 closing, $268,082 a year, $1.34 a foot a year, and 10.8% of Lee’s Q3 2026 IE West average NNN of $1.034. Termination is where the free rent comes back. Paragraph 13.3, Inducement Recapture, makes abated rent, landlord-paid tenant improvements and cash inducements conditional on full and faithful performance, so on a Breach that ends the lease every dollar abated falls due at once and a later cure does not reverse it. Filed leases show the market fix is an unamortized limit triggered only by default, which would cut the $800,000 to $500,000, but it sits in the abatement paragraph and does nothing about the ownership trigger in paragraph 12, which tenants read as a sublease question. The issue gives the six asks in the order to make them, with sample language for each, and a worked 200,000 square foot illustration.

Issue 10
Nov 6, 2026
Regulatory deep-dive

Perris voters approved a warehouse tax. Over 50,000 SF, your customer mix decides if you owe.

Perris voters approved a general business license tax of up to $0.10 a foot a year on non-e-commerce warehouse, industrial and manufacturing operators over 50,000 square feet with large truck activity: $30,000 a year on a 300,000 square foot building at the full rate, and $159,274 over five years if the rate rises at the 3% cap every year, which is $0.53 a foot over the term and 0.91% of Lee’s Q3 2026 IE East average NNN of $0.915 in year one. The city describes the tax as paid by operators and not as a property tax, so on that description it never reaches the Real Property Taxes paragraph a tenant negotiated and a tight tax clause does nothing about it. Three things keep a building out: under 50,000 square feet, no large truck activity, or an “E-commerce facility,” which the city defines as a building or separately identifiable portion principally used to handle goods ordered through an online platform for delivery directly to end consumers. That test looks at use and customers rather than at the lease, so a 3PL’s account mix, a sublease of part of the building or an assignment to a B2B user changes the answer while the lease stays the same, and paragraph 12.2(a) of the AIR single-tenant net lease keeps the original tenant primarily liable. The concept drew 52.16% as a special tax in 2023 and lost; as a general tax in 2026 it drew 79.50% on the semi-final count, because a general tax needs only a majority under Cal. Const. art. XIII C §2(b) while a special tax needs two-thirds.

Oct 30, 2026
Trade & supply chain

You paid for the switchgear. Your landlord keeps it, and new gear costs 89% more.

A tenant that spent $750,000 in January 2020 on an added electrical service and distribution for a 250,000 square foot IE West building leaves behind an installation that costs about $1,154,078 to replace today: $4.62 a foot, 4.5 months of rent at Lee’s Q3 2026 IE West average of $1.034, and more than the four months free that is the median on Lee’s 100,000 square foot renewals. The AIR single-tenant net lease classes power panels and electrical distribution as Utility Installations rather than Trade Fixtures, so under paragraph 7.4 the landlord either keeps them at expiration or orders them out, and the tenant repairs the removal damage either way. A restoration waiver protects against the cost and does nothing about the asset. The switchgear producer price index is up 89.8% since January 2020, Section 232 duties sit at 25% on the typical pad-mount distribution transformer, and switchgear lead times run near 44 weeks, so relocating means rebuying at 2026 prices on a clock longer than most moves allow.

Issue 08
Oct 23, 2026
Lease economics

Your landlord signed your blend-and-extend. If its lender didn’t, the rent cut can unwind.

A blend-and-extend that takes a 150,000 square foot Ontario building from $1.45 to $1.05 per square foot a month saves $720,000 in its first year. If the landlord’s lender never consented in writing and the loan fails before the original October 2029 expiry, the lender or whoever buys from it can treat the rent cut as never made: $0.40 a foot a month, $4.80 a year, $2,247,552 through 2029, or 14.3 months of the amended rent. The term that allows it is not in the lease or the amendment. It sits in the subordination, non-disturbance and attornment agreement signed years earlier, where lender forms commonly say a successor owner is not bound by amendments made without the lender’s written consent.

Submarket spotlight

Moreno Valley is redrawing your trucks’ route to the freeway. Your lease leaves you the bill.

Moreno Valley’s proposed truck route map, PEN26-0058, strips through-truck status from segments of nine streets, among them Perris Boulevard, Alessandro, Heacock, Frederick and Cactus, and concentrates trucks on SR-60 and I-215. The city’s own consultant puts the citywide result at 8,296 added truck miles a day, up 2.8%. On a 300,000 square foot building fronting Perris Boulevard with a quarter of its round trips heading east on SR-60, the added miles run about $294,000 a year, $0.98 a foot, 7.9% of IE East asking rent. The change takes no land, so the condemnation clause never fires, and paragraph 6.3 of the standard AIR lease leaves compliance with the tenant.

Regulatory deep-dive

AB 98 can turn your expansion option into a building the state won’t let you build

Since January 1, 2026, Government Code 65098 treats an addition of 20% or more of a warehouse’s non-office square footage as an expanded logistics development. On a 200,000 square foot building with 10,000 feet of office, the line sits at 38,000 square feet, so a 50,000 square foot expansion right crosses it at 26.3%. Add the result and the combined 250,000 square feet pulls in the Tier 1 standards and a 300-foot dock setback where homes sit within 900 feet. The illustrated fallback, a 50,000 square foot satellite eight miles away, runs $379,659 a year, $1.90 per square foot, 15% of IE West asking rent.

Thirty days to put your building in a foreign-trade zone. Your lease is what stops you.

A usage-driven foreign-trade zone site is approved in thirty days, and Riverside County and San Bernardino County are both inside an approved service area. The federal application runs on the tenant’s lease, and the standard Inland Empire use and alterations clauses do not carry it. The weekly-entry saving alone is $135,512 a year, or $0.90 per square foot, on a 150,000 square foot building.
