If your 250,000 SF-plus warehouse burns, nobody insures the year AB 98 can add.

AB 98 protects an existing warehouse of 250,000 SF or more from its 300-foot dock setback when homes go up within 900 feet, and says nothing about whether that protection survives a fire. On a 300,000 SF building, a 12-month redesign is $3.72 million of stopgap rent that your business-income policy excludes, and your lease stops abating rent when the landlord's rent-loss insurance runs out.

A 300,000 SF distribution center in South Ontario burns and has to be demolished. If homes were permitted within 900 feet of its docks after AB 98 took effect, and redesigning the rebuild around the statute adds 12 months, a tenant whose lease keeps it in place through restoration pays $3,722,400 of stopgap rent that no policy covers. That is $12.41/SF, a full year of rent at Lee's Q3 2026 IE West average NNN of $1.034, or $2.48/SF/yr spread over a five-year term. For those same 12 months the rent on the burned building comes back as well.

Your insurance and your lease were written to cover a fire, not a change in land-use law. The standard business-income form excludes any delay caused by an ordinance, and the AIR lease caps your rent abatement at whatever the landlord's rent-loss policy pays, so the year AB 98 adds lands on you twice.

The rule is Gov. Code 65098.1(a). Since January 1, 2026, any “proposed new or expanded logistics use development” of 250,000 SF or more on industrial land, with a loading bay within 900 feet of a sensitive receptor, must orient its truck bays away from receptors where feasible and locate them at least 300 feet from the nearest receptor's property line. Tier 1 building standards come with it. A home is a sensitive receptor under 65098(e).

The shield is 65098.1.5(a)(1): a logistics development “in existence as of September 30, 2024, shall not be subject to the requirements” if a new receptor is built or permitted later. Nothing in the AB 98 text or the 2025 cleanup bill as amended September 9, 2025 mentions reconstruction, rebuild, damage, destruction, fire or casualty. Whether a demolish-and-rebuild is a “proposed new” development that loses the shield is unresolved; no city determination, opinion or case answers it. A plan checker reading the statute plainly could treat a new building permit on a cleared pad as a new development. Repairing a standing building is unlikely to count.

That loss happens here. On April 7, 2026, fire destroyed the 1.2 million SF Kimberly-Clark distribution center near Eucalyptus Avenue and South Hellman Avenue in Ontario. NFI Industries, which ran the building, took 423,665 SF at 20123 Harvill Ave in Perris on an 18-month term at $0.83/SF NNN to keep the product moving (CoStar). Nothing ties that site to AB 98. It shows what the stopgap looks like.

Your business-income policy pays the extra expense of that stopgap only through the “period of restoration”, and ISO CP 00 30 says that period “does not include any increased period required due to the enforcement of any ordinance or law” that regulates construction, use or repair. The fix is ISO CP 15 31, Ordinance or Law – Increased Period of Restoration, which extends the period to cover compliance with any ordinance or law “in force at the time of loss.” AB 98's standards have been in force since January 1, 2026, so a 2027 fire meets the endorsement's timing test.

The AIR form makes the landlord insure the cost of code upgrades on its building. Nothing in it makes anyone insure the time.

The lease side is in the AIR single-tenant net form. ¶8.3(a), Building Improvements, lets the landlord's property policy cover the cost of complying with Applicable Requirements that force upgrading, demolition or reconstruction after a loss. ¶8.3(b), Rental Value, insures the landlord's rent for one year plus a 180-day extended period. ¶9.6(a), Abatement of Rent, abates your rent while the premises are impaired, but never beyond the Rental Value proceeds the landlord collects. ¶8.4, Business Interruption, requires your own income cover but sets no ordinance-or-law requirement. If the landlord's rent-loss policy is an ISO business-income form, it carries the same ordinance exclusion. Either way it stops at 18 months, against 26 months of downtime in the illustration, and your abatement stops with it.

Downtime = 14 mo code-free rebuild (BI pays) + 12 mo AB 98 redesign (BI excludes) = 26 mo
Landlord rent-loss cover = 12 mo + 180 days = 18 mo < 26 mo
Uncovered stopgap = 300,000 SF × $1.034 × 12 mo = $3,722,400

One condition governs the double rent. Unamended, the AIR form calls damage that cannot reasonably be repaired in six months a Premises Total Destruction (¶9.1(b)), and ¶9.4 ends the lease 60 days later. The two-rent case needs a lease that keeps you in place: a negotiated restoration-and-return clause, a landlord rebuild election on an institutional form, or a build-to-suit. Where the lease does terminate, the business-income gap remains: the policy stops paying at the code-free rebuild date whether or not you have found permanent space.

What to ask for, and when

1. Now, at your next insurance renewal, with no lease consent needed: add CP 15 31 to your business-income program, or the manuscript equivalent on a large-account form, with a period long enough to cover a site redesign, not plan check. CP 15 31 reaches ordinances that regulate “construction or repair”, while the exclusion also names “use”, and an insurer could argue AB 98 is a use law. Ask for wording that names land-use and siting laws.

2. At the next renewal, new lease or amendment, when ¶8, ¶9 and the insurance exhibit are open: make the abatement independent of the landlord's insurance. Sample language: “Rent shall abate for the entire period of restoration, including any increased period required to comply with Applicable Requirements in force at the time of loss, and such abatement shall not be limited by the amount of Rental Value insurance proceeds received by Lessor.”

3. Same document: require the landlord's ¶8.3(b) Rental Value cover to carry an ordinance-or-law increased-period endorsement and an indemnity period of at least 24 months.

4. Same document, where the lease keeps you in place through restoration: a termination right if restoration, including any redesign or re-entitlement required by AB 98 or other Applicable Requirements, is not substantially complete within a stated number of months, for example 15. Then you sign permanent space instead of paying two rents.

5. Before any loss: ask the landlord to seek a written determination from the city that rebuilding the existing building on the same footprint keeps its 65098.1.5 status, and to covenant to apply for any rebuild on that basis. Ask for the receptor map too: which homes within 900 feet were permitted after January 1, 2026.

6. On a signed lease with no renewal soon: make the insurance ask now and raise 2 through 4 at the next amendment, blend-and-extend or estoppel.

Billing error of the week: pro rata share on stale square footage

Illustration, on assumed figures: a 120,000 SF tenant in a business park that grew from 600,000 SF to 750,000 SF when a 150,000 SF building and its costs joined a $1,500,000 CAM pool ($2.00/SF/yr), still billed at its original 20% share instead of 16%. That is $60,000 a year, $0.50/SF/yr, 25% over its correct CAM, and $180,000 across a three-year audit lookback. The same error runs the other way when a building is sold out of the park or remeasured smaller.

Look at the tenant's share line at the top of the year-end reconciliation, which is usually printed once and applied to every pool. Check it against the share in your lease, your square footage divided by current park square footage from the landlord's rent roll or site plan, and the building areas on the county assessor's roll for every parcel. Then see whether last year's denominator moved.

AIR multi-tenant forms print Lessee's Share as a fixed percentage in the basic provisions. Ask for a ratio instead, premises RSF over total RSF of the buildings whose costs are in the pool, recalculated whenever a building is added, sold or remeasured, with access to the measurement backup.
