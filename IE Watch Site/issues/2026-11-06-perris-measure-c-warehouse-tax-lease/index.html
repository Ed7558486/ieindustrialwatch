This week · Regulatory deep-dive
Measure C · Business license tax · E-commerce status

Perris voters approved a warehouse tax. Over 50,000 SF, your customer mix decides if you owe.

Perris voters approved a business license tax of up to $0.10/SF on non-e-commerce warehouse, industrial and manufacturing operators over 50,000 SF with large truck activity: $30,000 a year on 300,000 SF at the full rate. The city says operators pay it, and on that description it never shows on your NNN reconciliation and a tight tax paragraph does nothing about it. Whether you owe it turns on what your building ships and to whom, and a customer shift, a subtenant or an assignee can change that answer.

On a 300,000 SF non-e-commerce distribution building in Perris, Measure C costs $30,000 a year once billing starts, at the full $0.10/SF/yr, and $159,274 over five years if the rate rises at the 3% cap every year. That is $0.53/SF over the term, and 0.91% of Lee's Q3 2026 IE East average NNN of $0.915/SF/mo in year one. It will not decide a move by itself. It passed with 79.50% of the vote on the semi-final count, and the size matters less than who gets the bill and what can change it.

The AIR single-tenant net lease does not allocate this tax. The city describes it as “paid exclusively by qualifying warehouse and industrial operators” and “not a property tax,” and in a single-tenant NNN building the operator on that description is you. On that reading the bill comes straight to the tenant, outside the Real Property Taxes paragraph you negotiated, and nothing in the lease says whose e-commerce status controls.

Three things keep you out: under 50,000 SF, no large truck activity, or an “E-commerce facility.” The city's definition covers a building or a “separately identifiable portion of a building” that is “principally used” to handle goods ordered through an online platform “for delivery directly to end consumers.” Store replenishment and B2B distribution do not fit that language on a plain reading. There is no city ruling yet, and “principally” carries no percentage in the public materials. KVCR reports Perris “holds five e-commerce warehouses” that generate $6 million a year in sales tax, and Mayor Pro Tem Malcolm Corona gave the flow of sales tax as a reason for the exemption, but the city's definition does not condition the exemption on where sales tax lands.

We read the city's measure page, the ballot question and the press, not the ordinance. Those materials do not settle the legal definition of “operator,” the start date, the first bill date, multi-tenant treatment, or whether the tax follows part of a building. The city's 2023 predecessor drew “large truck” at over 5 tons; check the 2026 line in the ordinance. The ballot question says the tax runs “until ended by voters.”

Tax = SF × up to $0.10, rising up to 3% a year
300,000 × $0.10 = $30,000 in year 1; five years at the 3% cap = $159,274

The rate is a ceiling. KVCR reports a council supermajority could reduce it, and the city words the increase as “capped at 3% (CPI adjusted),” so $159,274 is the top of the range, not a forecast. The status question is the one that moves. The test looks at use and customers, not at the lease, so a 3PL's account mix, a sublease of part of the building or an assignment to a B2B user changes the facts while the lease stays the same. Sublease half the building to a non-e-commerce user and, if the tax follows the portion, that is 150,000 SF × $0.10 = $15,000 a year, $79,637 over five years. Until this vote e-commerce status carried no business license tax consequence in Perris, so a sublease or assignment had no reason to address it. The AIR single-tenant net lease, ¶12.2(a), keeps you primarily liable after an assignment or sublease.

A tax billed to the operator never passes through ¶10, so a tight ¶10 does nothing about it.

Every competent IE broker protects you on Real Property Taxes. Here that negotiation is aimed at the wrong target, and a carve-out limited to gross receipts misses in the other direction too. The AIR single-tenant net lease, ¶10.1, Definition, reaches license fees levied on the landlord's interest or its leasing business. One filed AIR-form lease, for 119 Standard St. in El Segundo, carves out a business license tax “based on Lessor's gross receipts.” Measure C is measured by square footage and activity, so if a landlord were ever assessed it, that carve-out does not clearly apply. Whether the city could bill an owner is unknown. The fix is cheap protection, not a response to a known risk.

This reaches past Perris because of the vote math. A general tax needs a majority under Cal. Const. art. XIII C §2(b); a special tax needs two-thirds under §2(d). Perris ran the concept as a special tax in 2023, at up to $0.107/SF, drew 52.16% and lost. It ran it as a general tax in 2026 and drew 79.50%. §2(b) also ties the election to a regularly scheduled general election for the governing body, absent a unanimous emergency declaration, which in most cities puts the next attempt at November 2028. That date falls inside any five-year lease signed this winter.

What to do about it, in order

1. This month, if you occupy over 50,000 SF in Perris: classify the building. Ask the city in writing (warehousebusinesstax@cityofperris.org) for the ordinance, the start date, the first bill date and its reading of your operation. Do it before your next budget.

2. At signing or renewal in Perris, 12 to 18 months before expiry: add $0.10/SF, growing up to 3% a year, to the occupancy comparison against sites outside the city. Treat it as a line item and raise it in the rent discussion.

3. At the LOI for any lease or amendment, in any IE city: widen the ¶10.1 carve-out. Sample language: “Real Property Taxes exclude any business license, business activity or similar tax, however measured, including by square footage, truck activity or gross receipts, whether imposed on Lessor or Lessee, except to the extent Lessor proves it is a substitute for ad valorem tax on the Premises.” Expect pushback on the last clause. The core ask is “however measured.”

4. On the sublease or assignment term sheet: make e-commerce status a term. Sample language: “Sublessee shall obtain any business license and pay any tax imposed on its operations at the Premises, shall give Sublessor 30 days' notice of any change in use that alters the classification of the Premises or any portion under Perris Municipal Code section __, and shall indemnify Sublessor for any resulting tax on Sublessor.” ¶12.2(a) leaves you primarily liable, so do not rely on the assumption alone.

5. Before the first filing, if you are a 3PL: add a customer-contract line. Status depends on what your accounts ship, so a change in account mix belongs in your pricing review. Keep records of the share of throughput going directly to end consumers, because “principally” will have to be shown.
