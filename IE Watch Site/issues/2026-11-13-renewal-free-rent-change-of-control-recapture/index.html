Issue 11 · Lease economics · November 13, 2026

Sell your company without landlord consent and your free rent can come back in full.

Four months free on a 200,000 SF renewal is $800,000. On the AIR net lease forms that free rent is conditional, and a sale of your company, or a recapitalization that cuts its net worth by more than 25%, counts as an assignment unless the landlord consents in writing. The landlord can then call it a Breach with no cure period and choose between taking all $800,000 back and raising your rent 10% for the rest of the term.

On a 200,000 SF IE West renewal at Lee's 2026 medians for 100,000 SF+ deals, $1.00/SF/mo NNN, four months free and a 64-month term, the free rent is worth $800,000, or $4.00/SF. Sell the company in month 24 without the landlord's written consent and the AIR form gives the landlord two choices. It can terminate and collect all $800,000 back, with no amortization. Or it can raise rent to 110% for the remaining 40 months, which is $893,607: $268,082 a year, $1.34/SF/yr, 10.8% of Lee's Q3 2026 IE West average NNN of $1.034.

You never assign or sublet anything. The deal happens at the parent or the private-equity sponsor, and the AIR net lease forms, ¶12.1, Lessor's Consent Required, turn it into an assignment anyway. Unless your stock trades on a national exchange, a cumulative 25% shift in voting control needs the landlord's consent, and so does any merger, buyout or financing that cuts your net worth by more than a quarter.

The rest of the mechanism follows from there. Under ¶12.1(d) the landlord, not you, decides whether an unconsented transfer is a curable Default or a non-curable Breach, and a non-curable Breach needs no notice and no grace period. The landlord may then terminate, or on 30 days' notice lift Base Rent to 110% of the rent in effect, with every later scheduled increase also at 110%. ¶12.2(a) keeps you primarily liable regardless.

Termination is where the free rent comes back. ¶13.3, Inducement Recapture, in the AIR multi-tenant net form, makes abated rent, landlord-paid TI and cash inducements conditional on your full and faithful performance of the lease. On a Breach that ends the lease, every dollar abated is due at once, and curing the Breach afterward does not change that. One filed 2017 single-tenant copy runs from ¶13.2 straight to ¶13.4, so check whether yours carries it.

¶13.3 recapture = all abated rent = $800,000; a later cure does not reverse it
Negotiated version = $800,000 × 40 ÷ 64 = $500,000, after default and termination only
¶12.1(d) alternative = 10% × Base Rent, months 25–64 = $893,607

Brokers do negotiate free rent clawbacks, and filed leases show what the market settles on. Solid Power's lease in Thornton, Colorado makes “all unamortized Abated Rent (amortized on a straight line basis over the initial Term)” due, and only after termination for an uncured default. Every abated rent recapture in the Law Insider samples is default-triggered. That fix lives in the abatement paragraph. It does nothing about how a Breach gets created, and the trigger sits in ¶12, which tenants read as a sublease question. The change-of-control and net-worth tests are printed in the form and rarely touched for private companies.

On a dividend recap the tenant entity stays the same, nobody sells anything, and the landlord still has a deemed assignment.

The net-worth test is the one that catches people. A dividend recap or a leveraged refinancing that takes more than 25% off the tenant's net worth is an assignment the landlord may refuse. Asking for consent after closing does not undo a Breach the landlord has already elected and terminated on.

No landlord wants to lose a paying 200,000 SF tenant, so termination is the threat, not the forecast. The realistic outcome is a consent fee or a rent concession negotiated under it, with the 110% remedy as the credible lever. Either way it sets the price of the consent letter the buyer's counsel will need. The stakes are bigger than when most leases were last read: Lee's 2026 comps show four months free across 53 renewals over 100,000 SF and six months across 75 new leases. And tenants are trading. Capstone Partners counts 48 3PL deals nationally in 2026 through June, up 20% year over year, 23 of them sponsor-backed.

What to negotiate before you sign, in order

1. At the LOI or renewal, 12 to 18 months before expiration: carve ownership changes out of ¶13.3. Sample language: “No change in control, transfer of ownership interests, merger, recapitalization or financing affecting Lessee or any parent of Lessee shall constitute a Default or Breach for purposes of Paragraph 13.3, and Paragraph 13.3 shall apply only following termination of this Lease for Lessee's failure to pay Rent beyond applicable notice and cure periods.”

2. Same document: limit recapture to the unamortized balance. Sample language: “Any Inducement Provision recaptured under Paragraph 13.3 shall be limited to the unamortized portion thereof, amortized on a straight-line basis over the initial Term.” In the illustration below that takes the exposure from $800,000 to $500,000.

3. Same document: add a Permitted Transfer clause that covers sponsor events, not only affiliates. Sample language: “Lessor's consent shall not be required for (i) any transfer of direct or indirect ownership interests in Lessee, (ii) a merger, consolidation or sale of substantially all assets of Lessee or its parent, or (iii) any recapitalization or financing, provided the resulting tenant entity has a tangible net worth not less than $[X] and written notice is given within 30 days after closing.” Strike the ¶12.1(c) net-worth test, or replace “greater than 25%” with a fixed floor tied to the rent.

4. Same document: limit ¶12.1(d). An unconsented change in control should be a curable Default only, with 30 days after notice to deliver the consent request, and the 110% remedy struck for ownership-level transfers.

5. If the lease is already signed and a sale, sponsor exit or recap is possible in the next 24 months: put the lease on the deal checklist now. Request consent at least 60 to 90 days before a signed purchase agreement, not after closing, and ask for a written statement that the inducements under ¶13.3 are not affected. Get it into the estoppel. The 2017 single-tenant form, ¶12.2(e), asks for a written request with financial information and a $500 fee.

6. On any later sublease or assignment: have the consent agreement state that the abatement and any landlord-paid TI are not recaptured because of the transfer.
