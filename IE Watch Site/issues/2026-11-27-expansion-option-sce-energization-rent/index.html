Your expansion option delivers the space in six months. Edison takes sixteen to power it.

Your expansion option starts rent the day the landlord delivers the space. If the expansion needs new power, Southern California Edison's own data puts a new line and service at about 16 months from application to energization. On a 150,000 SF expansion delivered in six months, that is $1.58 million of rent on space that can't run.

A tenant exercises an option on 150,000 SF of IE West expansion space for EV truck charging, cold storage or automation, and the landlord delivers it six months later. The new load needs an SCE line extension and a new service, and SCE's own reported average for that job, from application to energization, is 338 business days, about 16.2 months. That leaves 10.2 months of rent at Lee's Q3 2026 IE West average NNN of $1.034: $1,577,131, or $10.51/SF, on space that can't do the work it was leased for. Spread over a five-year term it is $2.10/SF/yr.

Your expansion clause starts rent on delivery, and the landlord's power promise covers only the systems already in the building. Edison's clock for the load you are adding sits outside both, so the gap is yours unless the expansion amendment says otherwise.

SCE's energization report, filed with the CPUC on April 1, 2026, gives end-to-end averages measured from receipt of an application to energization, for applications from January 31, 2023 through December 31, 2025. A service extension alone under Rule 16 averaged 171 business days. A distribution line extension under Rule 15 averaged 480. The combination most industrial power upgrades need, a new line plus a new service, averaged 338, with a median of 292. Those totals include customer and third-party time for permits, easements and materials. That is the right measure for a tenant, who waits through all of it.

The CPUC set energization targets in D.24-09-020 in September 2024, but they cover only the steps SCE controls: a 125-business-day average. SCE's March 2026 report puts its own 2025 average for Rule 15 jobs at 235. The commission has since moved the proceeding into enforcement and third-party audits, with no penalties set. SCE's clock starts only when it deems the application complete, and Step 3 of its process puts permits, easements and owner documents on the customer. In a leased building that means the landlord's signature.

Filed leases show where the risk sits. A 2023 expansion amendment at 75 Hayden Ave in Lexington, Massachusetts makes the Expansion Premises Commencement Date “the date on which Landlord delivers the Expansion Premises to Tenant,” starts rent four months later, and promises base building systems in “good working order.” A September 2025 distribution lease at the Sugar Land Interchange in Texas warrants systems in “good operating condition and repair” and pushes commencement day-for-day for certificate-of-occupancy delay. Nothing in it moves for utility delay. Bloom Energy's lease of a 164,293 RSF building in Fremont lets the tenant add capacity as its own alteration, has the landlord “reasonably cooperate with Tenant” on utility applications, and says “the obtainment or provision of any such additional electrical capacity is not a condition or contingency to this Lease.”

338 bd = 492 calendar days = 16.2 mo; (16.2 − 6.0) × 150,000 × $1.034 = $1,577,131 (before rounding)

A competent broker negotiates the delivery condition, existing amperage, a delivery deadline with day-for-day extension, and free rent on the expansion. All of it is measured against the landlord's work. Edison's clock for your new load is measured against nobody's. Data-center leases tie commencement to power. On every industrial expansion clause found for this issue, nothing does. Buyers whose business depends on power already act on it: EV Realty confirmed 9 MW reserved from SCE before closing on 2151 E. Philadelphia St in Ontario, a purchase announced in September.

All of it is measured against the landlord's work. Edison's clock for your new load is measured against nobody's.

This applies only when the expansion needs power the building doesn't have: EV truck charging, cold storage, automation, manufacturing. An expansion that runs on the existing service has no gap.

What to do about it, in order

1. Nine to 12 months before the exercise deadline: get a load letter from your electrical engineer stating the added kW or kVA the expansion needs, and ask SCE for a preliminary read on whether the job is Rule 16 alone or Rule 15 plus Rule 16. That decides whether the gap is two months or ten.

2. In the exercise notice or expansion amendment: start expansion rent on the later of delivery and energization, with an outside date. Sample language: “Rent for the Expansion Premises shall commence on the later of (a) the Expansion Premises Delivery Date and (b) the date the serving utility energizes electrical service to the Expansion Premises of not less than [___] amps at [___] volts (the ‘Required Power’), provided Tenant submitted a complete application to the utility within [30] days after the Exercise Date and has diligently pursued it. If the Required Power is not energized within [___] months after the Delivery Date, Tenant may rescind its exercise as to the Expansion Premises.”

3. Same document: replace “reasonable cooperation” with deadlines. Owner authorization for the SCE application within 10 business days of request, easements for the transformer pad and service route within 30 days of SCE's design, and landlord delay counted day-for-day against expansion rent.

4. If the landlord won't move commencement: abatement equal to the SCE gap, capped, or a reduced storage-only rate on the expansion until energization.

5. If it won't do either: file the SCE application the day you exercise, push the delivery date out to match SCE's estimate, and negotiate a right to delay delivery.

6. Ask who is SCE's Applicant of record and who pays for the line extension. Issue 09 covered who keeps the Rule 15 refunds.

Billing error of the week: a new roof billed as one year's operating expense

Illustration, on assumed figures: a 100,000 SF tenant with a 40.0% share of a 250,000 SF multi-tenant building whose landlord replaces the whole roof at $8.00/SF, $2,000,000, the low end of one contractor's national range for a full TPO replacement. Billed in full in one year, the tenant's share is $800,000, $8.00/SF. Amortized over an assumed 20-year life, it is $40,000 a year, $0.40/SF/yr. The year-one overcharge is $760,000, 7.4 months of rent at Lee's IE West average. With three years left, the tenant should pay $120,000 for the roof in total.

Look at the repairs and maintenance or roof line of the year-end CAM reconciliation, sometimes labelled building repairs or capital. Compare it with last year's statement for a one-year jump, and ask for the invoices behind any line over a set dollar amount.

The operating-expense definition should exclude capital replacements, or include them only as an annual amortized installment over the item's useful life under GAAP, limited to installments inside your term, with interest capped at a stated rate. If your lease already says this, ask for a credit of the unamortized balance on the next reconciliation, within the audit deadline.
