This week · Submarket spotlight
Jurupa Valley · Prop 13

Your landlord's sale is a rent increase you never negotiated

Jurupa Valley put three deals on August's top-transaction sheet, and one of them was an investment sale. That sale is the one that changes what the building costs to occupy, for a tenant who had no part in it.

Three of the sixteen largest Inland Empire transactions in August landed in Jurupa Valley: US BIC Logistics expanded into 139,933 square feet at 11201 W Iberia Street, Continental Molding & Door Products signed 122,470 square feet at 10225 San Sevaine Way, and MDH Partners bought 118,644 square feet at 4388 Serrano Drive. Roughly 381,000 square feet across a lease, an expansion and a sale, in one city, in one month.

The sale is the one worth reading. Under Proposition 13, a California property's assessed value can rise no more than 2% a year, and it is reappraised at full market value only on a change in ownership or new construction. A building held ten years carries an assessed value anchored to what someone paid a decade ago. On the day it sells, that anchor is cut.

Assessed value = purchase price, then +2%/yr max
Change of ownership → reassessed at market

Inland Empire investment sales ran 8.7 million square feet in the first half of 2026, up 150% year over year, with dollar volume up 75.3% to $1.64 billion and cap rates compressed to 5.8% from 6.7%. Each of those closings reset a basis. A tenant on a triple-net lease pays its proportionate share of real property taxes, and standard lease language does not distinguish a tax increase caused by the landlord selling from one caused by the county raising rates. The tenant had no say in the sale and usually no notice of it. The first signal is the tax line on a reconciliation months later.

Jurupa Valley sharpens the arithmetic. Riverside County's combined rate typically runs 1.1% to 1.4% of assessed value, but Jurupa Valley is one of the jurisdictions where Mello-Roos districts push the effective rate past 1.5%. The same reassessment costs more here than it would in an older tax rate area.

What to negotiate before you sign

Ask for Prop 13 protection by name. The workable structures are well established: exclude the first reassessment during the term, cap the tenant's share of any increase by dollar amount or percentage, limit how often a reassessment can be passed through, or require the increase to be amortized over the remaining term rather than billed in the year it lands. Term length is the multiplier. A ten-year deal is more likely to contain a sale than a three-year deal, so the longer the lease, the harder this clause is worth fighting for. If the building has been held a long time and its assessed value sits far below what it would trade for, you are the one carrying that gap.

August did not give it back

The pull-forward ahead of the July 24 Section 301 duties was supposed to borrow from August. It mostly did not. Combined volume held at 1.88 million TEUs, up 0.8% year over year, with Long Beach loaded inbound up 3.6%. Two months of resilience is not a trend, but it removes the easy explanation that the summer was borrowed.

Regulatory and tax load on IE industrial tenants

In force · phasing · watch

AB 98 / SB 415 warehouse standards

State · Warehouse Concentration Region
Phasing in
Statewide siting, buffer, loading-bay and truck-route rules for logistics development. Access requirements began Jan 1, 2026; the truck-route deadline slipped to 2030 for smaller jurisdictions, with 2028 for the rest. San Bernardino County jurisdictions have largely adopted routes; Riverside County has made little progress. Enhanced standards near sensitive receptors trigger on zone changes or buildings over 250,000 SF. For tenants: constrains new modern supply near labor and rooftops, and a reconfiguration of an existing building can pull a project into the standards.

South Coast AQMD Rule 2305 (WAIRE)

Regional · Warehouses over 100,000 SF
In force
All three size phases are in annual reporting. Points are earned through zero- and near-emission equipment, chargers, or clean truck visits, or bought out at $1,000 per point plus 6.25% admin. For tenants: a recurring occupancy cost that belongs in the lease. Issue 01 costs it out at $0.40/SF for a 300,000 SF operator.

Tariff regime after the IEEPA ruling

Federal · Importers and 3PLs
In force
The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on Feb 20, 2026, leaving $166–179B in duties in refund litigation. The 10% Section 122 stopgap expired July 24 and was replaced the same day by Section 301 forced-labor tariffs on 60 economies — roughly 10% for the EU, Taiwan, Mexico, Canada and India; 12.5% for Japan, Korea, Vietnam, Thailand and Brazil; about 37.5% all-in for China. Section 232 metals stay at 50%. For tenants: landed-cost volatility drives inventory strategy, which drives square footage. Importers sitting on refund claims may have balance-sheet capacity that does not show in current credit.

Cap-and-invest tightening

State · Energy and fuel costs
In force
The tightened cap took effect September 1. CARB pulled 118 million allowances from future budgets, roughly an 11% cut to the cap through 2030 and about 7% a year to 2045, alongside a $4B manufacturing decarbonization fund. The August 2026 auction settled at $32.48 for current vintage allowances, up $3.67 from $28.81 in May, a 12.7% move in one quarter after years of settling near the floor. For tenants: allowance prices feed electricity and diesel costs, and a NNN utility clause that does not distinguish a regulatory surcharge from a rate increase passes the whole move through. Check whether yours does before modeling a 10-year occupancy pro forma.

Local moratoria and mitigation fees

Municipal · Entitlement risk
Watch
Individual cities continue to pause or restrict new warehouse entitlements, and Moreno Valley is negotiating an air-quality abatement fee with the Attorney General's Office that would have developers fund upgrades for nearby residents. For tenants: less new competitive supply near term, which supports rents in existing product; for owners, an entitlement path that is longer and costlier to underwrite.

Macro backdrop

Q2 2026 actuals and current bank forecasts
  • GDP grew 1.5% in Q2, below the 1.8% expected and down from 2.1% in Q1. Consumer spending carried it at a 3.2% annual clip; business investment ex-housing rose 8.4% on AI buildout.
  • Inflation is still running hot. Headline PCE +3.7% year over year, core +3.3%, both well above the Fed's target, with energy disruption from the Iran conflict feeding through.
  • Labor is tighter than the growth rate suggests. Private payrolls averaged 88,000 a month in the first half, roughly three times last year's pace; initial claims hit 187,000 in July, the lowest since 1969.
  • Wells Fargo reversed course in August, raising its inflation forecast and now expecting a quarter-point Fed hike before year-end, citing tariffs, elevated oil, and AI data-center power demand.
  • Bank of America has been the softer read, projecting core PCE near 3.1% through Q3 and 2.8% by Q4, easing toward 2.4–2.6% in 2027 — a path that supports cuts rather than hikes.
  • CoStar expects national industrial vacancy to peak this year, with rent growth projections unchanged through 2026.
The disagreement is the actionable part

Two of the largest research shops are pointed in opposite directions on the Fed. For owners weighing a 2027 refinance and tenants weighing renewal against relocation, that spread is worth more than either forecast on its own.

Why it matters here

Loaded imports, not total TEUs, are what convert into pallet positions in Fontana and Perris. Empties are roughly 37% of the combined count, and repositioning them tells you nothing about occupancy demand.
