This week · Regulatory deep-dive
AB 98 · Expansion options

AB 98 can turn your expansion option into a building the state won't let you build

Since January 1, 2026, California treats an addition of 20% or more of a warehouse's non-office space as an expanded logistics development. On a 200,000 SF building on industrial-zoned land with a 50,000 SF expansion right and homes within 900 feet, that can require every new dock to sit at least 300 feet from the homes' property line and leave you running a second building at about $380,000 a year. Your option was written before the rule and prices only rent.

On a 200,000 SF Fontana distribution building with a right to add 50,000 SF, AB 98 can cost you about $380,000 a year, $1.90/SF/yr. That is the cost of running a 50,000 SF satellite eight miles away when the addition your lease describes can't put its docks where the drawing shows them: 15% of IE West asking rent, every year, for growth you thought you had secured.

The trigger is a definition. Government Code 65098: “‘Expansion’ means the expansion of an existing logistics use development by 20 percent or more of the existing square footage. Office space shall not be included as part of the existing square footage or in the square footage for the 20-percent expansion threshold.” Take 10,000 SF of office out of a 200,000 SF building and the line sits at 38,000 SF. A 50,000 SF addition is 26.3%.

Expansion = Addition SF ÷ (Existing SF − Office SF) ≥ 20%
200,000 SF + 50,000 SF = 250,000 SF → 300-ft dock setback if any bay is within 900 ft of a home, school, daycare, park or hospital

Once the addition counts as an Expansion, the size of the result decides the tier. Government Code 65098.1(a) applies to “any proposed new or expanded logistics use development 250,000 square feet or more where the loading bay is within 900 feet of a sensitive receptor” on industrial-zoned land. That development must meet every Tier 1 warehouse design element, orient its bays away from the nearest receptor “to the extent feasible,” and “locate truck loading bays a minimum of 300 feet from the property line of the nearest sensitive receptor to the nearest truck loading bay opening using a direct straight-line method.” Tier 1 adds solar plus battery storage, a cool roof, truck-charging readiness, installed EV chargers on 10% of passenger spaces and zero-emission forklifts by January 1, 2028, to the extent operationally feasible.

Read on its text, the 250,000 SF test measures the expanded development, existing building plus addition. A 200,000 SF building with a 50,000 SF right lands exactly on the line. We found no Inland Empire city guidance on how it is measured. A planner who counts only the addition moves you to 65098.1(d), which requires orienting bays and routing trucks away from receptors and buffering light and noise, with no setback. That version costs more to build. The text-based version may not be buildable at all. On land not zoned industrial, 65098.1(c) sets a 500-foot setback across Riverside and San Bernardino Counties and named cities including Fontana, Ontario, Rancho Cucamonga and Moreno Valley.

The option survives on paper. The building it describes may not be permittable.

Nobody priced this because nobody had to. A competent broker negotiates the expansion rent, the notice window and the delivery date. Before AB 98, an addition on land already zoned industrial was a building-permit question, so no one re-read the option against siting law at renewal. The statute exempts “logistics projects that were subject to a commenced local entitlement process prior to September 30, 2024.” An expansion pad entitled as phase 2 of the original approval may qualify. A bare lease option is not an entitlement.

The lease decides who carries the gap, and the standard Inland Empire form puts it on you. AIR CRE's Standard Industrial/Commercial Single-Tenant Lease, ¶2.3(c), the same allocation First Industrial used with National RV at 100 West Sinclair Street in Riverside in 2007:

“If the Capital Expenditures are instead triggered by Lessee as a result of an actual or proposed change in use, change in intensity of use, or modification to the Premises then, and in that event, Lessee shall either: (i) immediately cease such changed use or intensity of use and/or take such other steps as may be necessary to eliminate the requirement for such Capital Expenditure, or (ii) complete such Capital Expenditure at its own expense. Lessee shall not, however, have any right to terminate this Lease.”

The same form measures compliance against the laws “in effect at the time that each improvement, or portion thereof, was constructed.” An addition built in 2027 answers to 2027 law. Tenant-built expansion rights typically let you build “at its cost” once you have “obtained all necessary governmental or municipal permits.” Landlord-built rights usually say the landlord “shall not be obligated to commence” until it receives approvals and “shall not be liable for any damage whatsoever” if it never delivers. Either way your remedy is the same: the option quietly lapses, and you find the space somewhere else.

What to do about it, in order

1. Now, or at least 18–24 months before your expansion notice window or renewal, run the division. Addition SF over existing SF minus office SF. At 20% or more, the addition is an AB 98 Expansion. Add it to the existing building. At 250,000 SF or more on industrial land, the 300-foot dock setback and Tier 1 apply.

2. At the same time, measure straight-line from every proposed new dock opening to the nearest residential, school, daycare, park or hospital property line. Under 900 feet, the statute is in play. Under 300 feet on a 250,000 SF result, the drawn expansion fails.

3. Ask the landlord in writing whether the expansion pad was inside a local entitlement process commenced before September 30, 2024. If it was, put the approval number into the lease as a landlord representation.

4. At renewal or any amendment, don't trade rent for an expansion option without an AB 98 rider. Sample language: “Landlord represents that, as of the Effective Date, the Expansion Building as depicted on Exhibit __ complies with Government Code Sections 65098 through 65098.6 as then in effect, or is exempt as a project subject to a local entitlement process commenced before September 30, 2024. If any Applicable Requirement in effect when Tenant delivers its Expansion Notice (i) requires a reduction of more than 10% in the Expansion Building’s square footage or dock count, or (ii) increases Landlord’s projected construction cost by more than $__ per square foot, then Tenant may, at its election, (a) accept a reconfigured Expansion Building with Expansion Rent reduced proportionately, (b) rescind its Expansion Notice without penalty, or (c) require Landlord to deliver comparable space within __ miles on the same rent and term, with Landlord reimbursing Tenant’s reasonable dual-site operating costs for the remaining Term. Costs of compliance with Government Code Section 65098.1 shall not be a Capital Expenditure triggered by Lessee under Paragraph 2.3(c).”

5. If the option prices expansion rent off construction cost, cap the Tier 1 increment or exclude it from the cost base.

6. Last resort: size the addition under 20% of existing non-office space, 37,999 SF on a 190,000 SF base, and phase the rest.

Worked illustration

200,000 SF Fontana distribution building with a 50,000 SF expansion right

Assumptions, not survey figures: industrial-zoned land, 10,000 SF of office, lease signed 2022 with a right to add 50,000 SF of warehouse on excess land. The fallback is a 50,000 SF satellite 8 miles away at the same rent as the expansion. Eight shuttle round trips a day, 16 miles each, 260 days, at ATRI’s $2.336 per mile. Regulatory and tax load on IE industrial tenants

In force · phasing · watch

AB 98 / SB 415 warehouse standards

State · Warehouse Concentration Region
Phasing in
Statewide siting, buffer, loading-bay and truck-route rules for logistics development. Access requirements began Jan 1, 2026; the truck-route deadline slipped to 2030 for smaller jurisdictions, with 2028 for the rest. San Bernardino County jurisdictions have largely adopted routes; Riverside County has made little progress. Enhanced standards near sensitive receptors trigger on zone changes or buildings over 250,000 SF. For tenants: constrains new modern supply near labor and rooftops, and an addition to a building you already occupy can pull the whole development into the standards. Issue 06 runs the 20% test and costs out an expansion option that cannot be built where the lease drew it.

South Coast AQMD Rule 2305 (WAIRE)

Regional · Warehouses over 100,000 SF
In force
All three size phases are in annual reporting. Points are earned through zero- and near-emission equipment, chargers, or clean truck visits, or bought out at $1,000 per point plus 6.25% admin. For tenants: a recurring occupancy cost that belongs in the lease. Issue 01 costs it out at $0.40/SF for a 300,000 SF operator.

Tariff regime after the IEEPA ruling

Federal · Importers and 3PLs
In force
The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on Feb 20, 2026, leaving $166–179B in duties in refund litigation. The 10% Section 122 stopgap expired July 24 and was replaced the same day by Section 301 forced-labor tariffs on 60 economies — roughly 10% for the EU, Taiwan, Mexico, Canada and India; 12.5% for Japan, Korea, Vietnam, Thailand and Brazil; about 37.5% all-in for China. Section 232 metals stay at 50%. For tenants: landed-cost volatility drives inventory strategy, which drives square footage. Importers sitting on refund claims may have balance-sheet capacity that does not show in current credit.

Cap-and-invest tightening

State · Energy and fuel costs
In force
The tightened cap took effect September 1. CARB pulled 118 million allowances from the 2027–2030 budgets, roughly an 11% cut to the cap through the end of the decade and about 7% a year to 2045, alongside a $4B manufacturing decarbonization fund. The August 2026 auction settled at $32.48 for current vintage allowances, up 12.7% from $28.81 in May. CARB’s own modeling of the amended program projects a weighted-average allowance price near $68 against a $44 baseline. California, Québec and Washington signed a linkage agreement on June 25 for joint auctions, expected to start between late 2027 and early 2028. For tenants: allowance prices feed electricity and diesel costs, and a NNN utility clause that does not distinguish a regulatory surcharge from a rate increase passes the whole move through. A doubling from here is the regulator’s own central case, not a bear case. Check the clause before modeling a 10-year occupancy pro forma.

Local moratoria and mitigation fees

Municipal · Entitlement risk
Watch
Redlands is furthest along. Its Planning Commission recommended Ordinance Text Amendment No. 372 on May 12 by 5–1, and on October 6 the City Council introduced Ordinance No. 3002 on a 5–0 vote, prohibiting new warehouses of 50,000 SF or more, or with more than five truck docks, in every zoning district. The council struck the provision that would have let existing warehouses add truck docks. An existing building may still request height up to its zoning maximum, generally 50 feet, by conditional use permit and with an energy audit showing an efficiency gain; the earlier 15% height cap and the economic-feasibility study requirement are gone. The ordinance returns for final adoption at a later meeting, with no date set. Beaumont, Moreno Valley and Fontana have each rejected recent industrial proposals, and Moreno Valley is negotiating an air-quality abatement fee with the Attorney General’s Office. For tenants: in Redlands the dock-addition path is now closed outright rather than discretionary, so an expansion that depends on new docks in that city fails on the current draft regardless of what the lease says. Elsewhere, an expansion option stops being a right and becomes a discretionary approval with environmental review attached. If your growth plan depends on adding docks or cube to the building you are in, confirm the city will still permit it before you commit to the term.

Macro backdrop

Q2 2026 actuals and current bank forecasts
  • The Fed raised rates on September 16, moving the target range to 3.75%–4.00% on a unanimous 12–0 vote. The statement says inflation “remains elevated” and frames the increase as support for “a timelier return” to the 2% goal.
  • Wells Fargo called it. It moved in August to forecast a hike before year-end, citing tariffs, elevated oil and AI data-center power demand. Bank of America had core PCE easing to 2.8% by Q4, a path that implied cuts. September settled the argument in Wells Fargo’s favor, and the day after the hike Wells Fargo put the funds rate at 4.00%–4.25% by year-end with no changes through 2027, on the reasoning that the excess inflation is supply-driven and the labor market is not overheating.
  • GDP grew 1.5% in Q2, below the 1.8% expected and down from 2.1% in Q1. Consumer spending carried it at a 3.2% annual clip; business investment ex-housing rose 8.4% on AI buildout.
  • Inflation is why. Headline PCE +3.7% year over year, core +3.3%, both well above target, with energy disruption from the Iran conflict feeding through.
  • Labor is tighter than the growth rate suggests. Private payrolls averaged 88,000 a month in the first half, roughly three times last year’s pace; initial claims hit 187,000 in July, the lowest since 1969. Locally the picture is softer: Inland Empire unemployment is 5.5% and the labor force shrank 1.0% over twelve months.
  • CoStar’s September 24 read on the Inland Empire puts vacancy at 8.7% for 2026 Q3 and availability at 12.5%, a 15-year high, against a 6.2% historical average vacancy. Trailing 12-month net absorption is 6.0M SF versus a prior 10-year average of 17.5M SF, deliveries 7.7M SF, and market asking rent growth −0.9%. Construction underway is 14.0M SF, down from the 45.7M SF peak in 2022, with less than 10M SF forecast for completion across 2026 and 2027.
What a hike does to the renewal question

Relocation is capital. Racking, conveyor, dock equipment, moving the operation, the months of duplicate rent: most of it gets financed, and financing just got more expensive rather than less. That strengthens the case for negotiating hard where you already sit and weakens the cheap-capital case for moving. For owners it cuts the other way on a 2027 refinance, which is the same pressure that makes a landlord willing to trade on rent to keep a covenant in place.

Why it matters here

Loaded imports, not total TEUs, are what convert into pallet positions in Fontana and Perris. Empties are roughly 37% of the combined count, and repositioning them tells you nothing about occupancy demand.
